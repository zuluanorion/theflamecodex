LIGHT CODE MEDITATION

A simple and deep meditation

Meditation has been at the centre of my life, and I have had the privilege of initiating thousands of people into it. I used to teach it with the bija mantras, the seed sounds of the tradition of India. Today I share it with the syllables of the Flame Codex.

Each syllable is chosen for what it is and what it does, not because it sounds like another. It is a door to a quality that already lives in you: serenity, clarity, rootedness, devotion.

The practice is simple. You begin with an intention. You look at the plate, if you have it. You let the syllable keep you company. And then you rest in silence. What you contemplate there, you carry afterwards into your life.

The crown receives. The heart translates.
The root incarnates. Life demonstrates.

WHAT YOU NEED

Nothing special

A quiet place, a few minutes and a sincere intention. That is enough to begin. The practice brings together six simple things:

Intentionwhat you wish to contemplate
Syllableone key of the Codex
Plateits image, if you use it
Body and breathyour support
Silencewhere you rest
Your lifewhere it all comes together
The syllable opens a door. Contemplation makes its quality familiar.
Silence gives it room. And your daily life makes it real.

WHY MEDITATE THIS WAY?

To relate differently to what you live

To learn to look at your thoughts, emotions and sensations without believing you are each one of them. And so that qualities like these can find a place in your life:

Presence · Clarity · Serenity · Discernment · Devotion · Rootedness · Receptivity · Will · Integration

And, with time, to let go of what weighs on you: energy that has stopped flowing, memories the body still holds, thoughts that keep repeating. When that loosens, something in you can breathe again.

Its meaning becomes concrete in small things: listening with more attention, answering with more clarity, holding a limit, acting with more coherence.

In the language of the Codex we say this practice prepares the human vessel so that the Flame, the Divine Intelligence entering incarnation, can express itself. But the Descent belongs to Grace: no technique and no syllable can guarantee it. When the practice speaks of making room for the descent, I mean a willingness: allowing what you contemplate to find body and expression. It does not mean that each session produces a new Descent.

YOUR SUPPORTS

Intention, syllable, gaze and the Observer

THE INTENTION

What do you wish to contemplate?

Before you begin, recognize it and say it simply, in harmony with what the syllable you chose does:

“I want to be present.”
“I want to accompany this emotion with serenity.”
“I want to see my next step clearly.”
“I want a more intimate relationship with the sacred.”

Once recognized, you do not need to repeat it: let it rest in the silence too. Intention gives you a direction; it does not oblige you to get a result.

THE SYLLABLE

A sound that keeps you company

You can say it softly or remember it inwardly, as its study page indicates. Pronounce it with care and without strain. You do not have to hold it with force the whole session: when it grows faint, let it rest in silence. If you get lost in your thoughts, remember it gently and rest again. There is no required number of repetitions.

THE PLATE

An image for the gaze

Look at it softly, without straining your eyes or analysing every detail. You can contemplate it before closing your eyes or during part of the session. If the image disappears when you close them, that is fine: carry on with the intention, the syllable, or simply being present. And if you have no plate, sound and presence are enough.

The plate is for contemplating, not for taking a trace from. When a practice includes tracing, the key’s original sign and its verified route are used.

THE OBSERVER

The awareness that notices

I call the Observer that awareness in you that notices what appears: thoughts, emotions, sensations, memories, images, sounds, silences. Observing is not policing every thought: it is noticing, and kindly coming back when you get distracted. And you do not have to feel separate from your body or your emotions: presence includes them too.

A SESSION, STEP BY STEP

The six scenes

This is what a session looks like, in six simple moments that accompany the five stages of the method.

1The meetingArrive, feel your body and get to know the key.
2ContemplationLook at the plate softly.
3The soundSay or remember the syllable as indicated.
4Turning inwardLet attention rest and the sound grow subtle.
5SilenceRest, without demanding that thoughts disappear.
6IntegrationCome back, and carry one quality into your life.

THE METHOD

Five stages

  1. Recognize the qualityBe clear about your intention and the syllable you chose. Notice how you arrive, how your body is. You can begin even if thoughts or emotions are present.
  2. Open the inner relationshipLet attention gather. Sound, breath and presence grow simpler. If you get distracted, come back gently.
  3. Make room for the descentLet the quality you contemplate touch your human, bodily experience: from the mind to the heart, to vitality, to the root and to the body. You do not have to visualize or feel something in each place.
  4. Observe and allowGive room to what appears. The syllable may grow faint: rest without chasing it. If an emotion arises, recognize it without forcing it to grow. If something becomes too much, open your eyes, look around you and stop the practice.
  5. Return and integrateFeel your hands, your feet, your breath and the sounds of the place again. Move gently and open your eyes at your own pace. Integration continues when what you contemplated becomes a gesture in your day.

BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Begin from where you are

Find a corner where you can be quiet for a few minutes. If you use a plate, place it at a comfortable distance; if it is on a screen, lower the brightness so it does not tire your eyes. Sit on a chair or a stable support, back upright but not rigid, hands resting, feet or seat as your support. No posture should hurt. Recognize your breath as it comes and soften your face and shoulders. Preparing is simply recognizing how you are and allowing yourself to begin from there.

How long?

Begin small and regularly. Five or ten minutes is a good start; fifteen or twenty if you feel comfortable. These times do not measure your depth and are not a requirement. Give yourself a moment to arrive and a minute or two to return. A fairly fixed time and place help. Constancy grows by coming back, not by competing.

Silence

Silence is the heart of this meditation. The syllable orients and then leaves room; it does not have to be held by force for the session to have meaning. There can be thoughts and sounds and, even so, rest. When you notice you have gone off after a mental story, return once to the sound or the body, and rest again. That is how attention learns to come back without fighting itself.

THE FIRST SYLLABLE

AHR · ÁR

Opening and anchoring of the primordial Flame. In the Codex it relates to the centres of the root, the heart and the crown. In the book I use it to teach you how to prepare the body, contemplate, bring in the sound and rest. To begin:

  • Remember ÁR inwardly during a soft inhalation.
  • Let any pause be natural.
  • As you exhale, let your attention go along the spine down to where your body rests.
  • Do not force your throat or hold your breath.
  • Then leave room for silence.

This guidance belongs to AHR; each syllable has its own.

CHOOSING THE SYLLABLE

Where do I begin?

Begin with your life, not with the list. Ask yourself: What needs attention right now? Do I need clarity, serenity, direction, receptivity, rootedness, or a more conscious way of relating to what I feel? Then look at the study page of that syllable and talk it over with whoever accompanies you. The first practice uses AHR, to learn the way.

FROM ONE SYLLABLE TO A TRIAD

Your first triad: settling into deep stillness

Once a simple practice feels familiar, you can get to know the combinations. The first one I teach, for settling into deep stillness, is:

IAHRCore · ÁR

Opens and orients: opening and anchoring.

→
IIDRO’VALSupport · DRO-VAL

Holds: foundation, rooting and consolation.

→
IIIZEN’LUMSeal · SEN-LUM

Closes: stillness and completion.

In the guide you become familiar with its sound in that order, with comfortable pauses, and then you rest. This is different from the full ritual with traces: when the traced invocation is performed, the verified signs of the three keys are used, with their own instructions. Triads are studied with accompaniment, respecting the role of each key.

WHAT MAY HAPPEN

Every session is different

There may be stillness, distraction, emotion, sensations in the body, or a session that feels completely ordinary. No phenomenon proves you went deeper.

If many thoughts come

Notice that you are thinking, and come back kindly to the support you chose.

If you cannot see the plate inwardly

Carry on with the syllable, the intention, or simply the presence of your body.

If you feel nothing special

Write the session down as it was. It is not a failure, and there is no need to imagine invisible effects you cannot observe.

If images or messages appear

You can write them down as inner experiences. They are not orders you have to obey.

If an emotion comes up

Give it room, as much as you can hold, without pushing it into a catharsis.

If it becomes too much

Open your eyes, look around you and stop. Ask for accompaniment if you need it.

AFTER MEDITATING

Meditation continues in your day

When you finish, you can ask yourself: What did I observe? What meaning do I give it? What simple gesture could express today the quality I contemplated? For example:

Claritysay something honestly
Serenitylisten without hurrying to answer
Rootednesstake care of one concrete task
Devotionoffer a gesture of gratitude or care
Discernmentrecognize a decision that needs more information, or a clear limit

Let it be something possible and concrete. You do not need to repeat the syllable all day: the quality can stay alive after the sound has ended.

It helps to tell what you observe from what you interpret. “I felt warmth in my chest” is a sensation; “I think it has to do with an emotion” is an interpretation. That way you recognize changes without crediting everything to the practice.

ACTIVATION AND ACCOMPANIMENT

When you want to go deeper

The basic meditation and the observation I teach in the book are open to everyone. When you wish to practise formally with a code, the path begins with its activation, received from a Guardian or Bearer of the Flame authorized to transmit it. Looking at the image or learning the sound does not replace it, and activation is not measured by how intensely you feel something.

Accompaniment helps you know which syllable to begin with, its intention, how to pronounce it, its particular indications, how to continue, and what to do with the questions or difficulties that come up. Meanwhile, you can study the material and continue with your basic meditation.

FLAME BEARERS AND FLAME KEEPERS

Who can accompany you

You can begin on your own, with a book and a quiet corner. But walking with someone who already knows the way makes everything simpler and deeper. In the Codex we speak of two kinds of companions:

FLAME BEARERS

Bearers of the Flame

People in whom the Flame is already incarnating. They live the process of the Descent in their own body and life, and accompany you from that lived experience. No certificate makes a Bearer: the Descent does.

FLAME KEEPERS

Keepers of the Flame

People who have prepared themselves and are attuning, so that this same process awakens in them. They care for the language of the Codex with faithfulness, ethics and love.
