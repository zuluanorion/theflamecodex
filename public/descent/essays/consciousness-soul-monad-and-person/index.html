Consciousness, soul, Monad and person

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch VI · The architecture of the Dwelling

The Descent · the essaysVI · The architecture23 / 42
“One Consciousness. Many eternal individualities. None isolated. None erased.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro de la Morada

You look at a photograph from thirty years ago and recognize a body that no longer exists in the same way. The face was smoother, the hair different, and the gestures were still learning to be yours. And yet something in you, as you look at it, does not say: “that was someone else.” It says: “that was me.”

The body changes. The personality changes. Your opinions may become unrecognizable to the person you were. And still, when you ask without words, “is anyone here?”, the presence you answer from does not feel wrinkled.

I do not offer this to you as proof, but as a possible experience. Some people recognize it with great clarity. Others do not live it that way, and they lack nothing. This teaching starts from that intuition, but it will not turn it into an exam.

The consciousness that does not age

The body accumulates years. Consciousness does not find itself made of age.

This does not mean that the body does not age, or that memory or attention stay intact. It names another dimension of experience: that by which any change can be known.

It helps to distinguish it from attention, which moves: now it is on these words, then on a noise, then on a thought. Consciousness is the field in which those movements appear. Nor is it the mind, which thinks, compares, remembers and anticipates. Even the sentence “I don’t know who I am” appears before something that knows it.

In the vision of this Yoga, consciousness is not inside the body like a lamp shut in a box. The body, the mind and the world appear in it and as part of it. And here I want to take care of you. Sometimes “watching yourself from outside” is not a contemplative metaphor but an experience of unreality, or of disconnection from your own body. If it becomes persistent, if it distresses you or interferes with your life, it deserves professional attention. The consciousness I am speaking of does not take you away from experience. It makes it more intimate.

But if consciousness is indivisible, who loves, learns, chooses and lives through a concrete story? To answer you I need a few old words: soul, Monad and person.

The soul and its Monad

The soul is the eternal individuality of consciousness: an unrepeatable center of experience, relationship and participation in the Divine. It is not a fragment cut from a whole, nor an isolated bubble. Think of a drop: it has its own path and can be recognized as this drop, but it never stopped being water. It is individuality without exile, an eternal way for Consciousness to experience, to love and to respond.

Eternal does not mean that your present personality has always existed. Your name, your accent, your tastes and your particular way of defending yourself belong to this life. What is eternal is the deep individuality, able to live many forms without being reduced to any of them.

Inseparable from the soul is the Divine Monad: the aspect of Divine Intelligence, purpose and transcendent knowing of that same individuality. It is not a supervisor placed over you, nor a more powerful second self waiting to replace you, nor an intermediary between the soul and God. It is the divine pole of who you already are. Its purpose is more like an essential orientation than a calendar of events: a compass has a north without deciding every stone on the path. And purpose does not mean that every abuse or every illness was written in advance to teach you a lesson.

Soul and Monad are co-eternal. There was never a soul without a Monad, waiting to receive its divine part, nor a Monad that at some point manufactured a soul. That is why you do not need to obtain your Monad: it was never missing. And you do not have to deserve it: it does not appear as a prize for purification, obedience or suffering.

Its knowing, however, does not make your personality omniscient. What reaches it passes through your language, your memory, your desire and your fear, and it can be true, partial, mixed or mistranslated. That is why discerning is not a failure of faith. The Monad can orient. The person still has to think.

The person, a surface of meeting

Your personality was formed with a body, a family, a language, an era, encounters, losses and decisions. It learned which emotions could be shown and which role seemed to give you a place. None of that is eternal in that same form. And none of it is irrelevant.

I call the person the local, temporal and embodied organization through which the soul lives a concrete existence. The soul does not speak Spanish from eternity; it speaks it through a person who learned it. The soul’s love does not cook a soup without hands. The person buys the ingredients, remembers how someone likes it and stays beside the pot.

Temporal does not mean false. Local does not mean insignificant.

Nor do I use the word ego as a synonym for wickedness. It is the function that lets you say this is my body, this is my responsibility, this limit needs my care. It can seize the throne and believe it is the whole. It can also be the good steward of a house whose owner has already been recognized. A window does not need to break to let in more light; it needs to become cleaner. And an overwhelmed personality does not need to be told it is an illusion. It needs safety, connection, rest and, when it is called for, professional care.

Misplaced words have consequences. If you call your personality your soul, a habit can disguise itself as spiritual destiny. If you call any intuition your Monad, a human preference shows up in a divine uniform. If you call the person an illusion, you may neglect its body, its bonds and its obligations.

What continues

The doctrine of this Yoga holds that the same soul, inseparable from its Monad, passes through many incarnations, while the local personality does not remain intact. I set it out for you clearly, but not as a proof. In this doctrine, the soul assimilates what a life came to integrate: wisdom, capacities, relationships and tendencies still open.

I sum it up this way: continuity without photocopy, assimilation without erasure, mystery without abandonment.

Here an absolute protection is needed. That something can be assimilated does not mean it was chosen, deserved or sent to teach you something. Harm is still harm. And none of this does away with grief. Using eternity to hurry grief is another way of failing to accompany human life. You can believe deeply in continuity and miss someone down to your bones. Both fit.

Continuity does not let you postpone, either. You cannot leave for another incarnation the repair that is in your hands today. The idea of continuity should give you more tenderness for time, not less.

Many windows, one light

Each soul is an eternal individuality, and none is ultimately separate from Consciousness or from the others. Think of many windows open to the same light: the light does not fragment as it passes through them, and the windows do not become the same window either.

An ethics is born from this. If the other is a real individuality, their consent matters. If they are not ultimately separate, their wellbeing is not foreign to you. The Dwelling looks for this gesture: “you are not me, you do not belong to me, and yet your existence concerns me.” That is why feeling unity with someone does not give you knowledge of their inner life. You may still need to ask.

And humility is born from it too. You can be inseparable from your Monad and not know what will happen tomorrow. You can recognize indivisible Consciousness and need eight hours of sleep. You can love deeply and need distance. None of that contradicts the doctrine. It embodies it.

The limited person is not the error in the system. It is the place where eternity consents to become concrete.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

If you have a photograph of yourself from many years ago at hand, look at it calmly.

Who recognizes it? Has that which is noticing, right now, that you are looking at it grown old?

And if you find nothing special, leave it there; no one is asking you to find it.
