From the Monad to the Flame

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch VI · The architecture of the Dwelling

The Descent · the essaysVI · The architecture24 / 42
“The drop is not the ocean in quantity. It is ocean in nature.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro de la Morada

If you ever feel coolness in your legs, pressure in your chest or a silent expansion at the base of your body, I ask something of you before you give it a big name. What you are living deserves to be described before a doctrine absorbs it. The language of this book can offer you a reading. It cannot take ownership of your sensation or decide for you what happened.

This part of the Descent is born from that care. In the book I have already distinguished Consciousness, soul, Monad and person. Now I come to a word I deliberately left at the threshold: Flame. And a simple question appears. If the Monad is the pole of Divine Intelligence inseparable from the soul, wouldn’t it be enough to call everything that happens Monad? Why add another word?

To distinguish is not to divide

We can distinguish two aspects of a single reality without turning them into separate things. If I distinguish the Monad from the Flame, it is not because I imagine two entities, but because I need to speak to you of two relationships of one and the same reality: its transcendent depth and its conscious manifestation in form.

Without that distinction, the confusions are easy to foresee. A sensation of heat ends up being called Monad, a current in the body is taken for the essence itself, and the word Flame ends up meaning, all at once, a sensation, an energy, an identity, a symbol, a mechanism and a mission. When a word means everything, it stops helping us see.

The Monad is transcendent, and here the word means something very precise: not exhausted by the temporal form. It does not live in a place above your head, nor does it watch you from a remote dimension. It is the ground of your whole individuality, even though it does not fit inside your biography. And its knowing is not data. You can feel an orientation and translate it badly, mix it with your fear, your desire, your memory or your imagination. The depth of the origin does not remove the fragility of the translation. That is why the sentence “my Monad told me” closes no conversation.

A drop of the same water

The Flame is the living manifestation of the essence of the Divine Monad when that essence becomes consciously embodied in form. It does not come to you from outside like a force hired to improve you, nor is it an entity taking up residence in someone else’s body. What descends is not something added to who you are. It is the subtlest of who you are becoming livable in form.

To say it with an image: the Monad plays the part of the ocean, because the Flame does not exhaust its depth, and the Flame plays the part of the drop, because it makes that same nature accessible to form. A drop of water does not hold all the waves or all the currents of the ocean, but it does not need to become another substance to belong to it. Its salt is not borrowed. The image has its limit: the divine essence does not divide like a quantity, and the Monad does not lose a piece every time the Flame manifests.

That image frees you from a spirituality of begging. The drop does not have to convince the ocean to lend it its nature. But that does not mean your personality can declare that everything it wants is monadic will. The drop is water; the glass still has edges.

That is why the Flame can be felt, but it is not defined by a sensation. Heat is not the Flame. Coolness is not the Flame. Bliss is not the Flame. They are possible ways in which the house responds. If something you feel in your body worries you, does not go away or keeps you from functioning, have it looked at first of all as what it is, an experience of your body. And if you feel nothing you know how to name, you are not left out. The Flame can become visible in a more whole decision, in a new capacity to love without disappearing, in a body that returns to calm sooner.

Essence, movement and bridge

The whole map fits in five lines. The Monad is the essence. The Flame is the manifestation. The Descent is the movement. The bridge is the function. Incarnation is the fulfillment. They are not five substances. They are five questions.

The movement is observed with humility. We do not have a demonstrated mechanism of the Flame, nor can we point to an anatomical structure along which the essence travels. One person feels something coming down from the crown of the head toward the heart, the base and the legs. Another first lives an opening of the heart and, years later, a greater presence in the body. Another recognizes a clarity with no direction in space. Sometimes, at the beginning, it feels like hot fire, and later like coolness, stability or a silent bliss. I do not turn that passage into a rule. The essence does not increase because the sensation increases.

The bridge, on the other hand, is recognized in life. Clarity becomes decision. Love becomes the way you treat others, and a clean limit. Will becomes action without violence. Wisdom becomes measure. And here there is a care to keep: the bridge does not authorize anyone to present themselves as a necessary mediator between the Divine and others. A bridge does not own those who cross it.

Why speak of a Descent

We arrive at the central paradox. If the Divine was never absent, what could descend? The answer lies in distinguishing the presence of being from presence that is consciously lived. You can touch the Divine in moments of Grace and then go back to an organization of your life in which almost nothing of that recognition takes part.

The Monad does not descend to be where it was not. It descends to be consciously known there, as there and through there.

Think of a house at dawn. The light is not born when you open a curtain. It was already there. But for the room, something real happens when it can receive it. That is why to descend does not mean to travel from somewhere else. A sensation that comes down can be true as an experience, and that does not turn it into a theory of space. If it comes with pain, weakness, loss of sensation, fainting, confusion or other worrying symptoms, set the spiritual map aside and seek a medical evaluation. The symbolic direction never takes priority over your health.

Nor does the Descent mean that the Monad lacks something. In this doctrine, the movement is born of the divine capacity to know itself and to express itself in form, relationship, limit and matter. I call that love: the depth that needs nothing and yet consents to become intimate with density.

In the first volume I spoke to you of a luminous guest who arrives, transforms the atmosphere and seems to withdraw. Now I can gently correct that image. The guest was never entirely a guest. It was the house’s own depth becoming perceptible. That is why I ask you not to keep watch over the Flame, and not to repeat practices out of fear of losing it. Something settles when it finds conditions in which to live, not when it is held by force.

At home in the root

The most concrete question of the Descent is this: can the Divine also live where life weighs? Because it can be felt in the heart during prayer and still be absent from your relationship with your body, with money, with desire or with the fear of dying. I put it this way: “The root is not the place farthest from God. It is the place where the idea of distance can finally fall.”

A truly rooted spirituality pays, cares, repairs, rests, manages, eats and recognizes consequences. It does not demand that your body stop feeling fear. It lets fear exist without automatically becoming sovereign.

All of this comes together in a law that will have to remain intact when techniques, syllables and practices appear:

The Flame is not energy trying to reach God. It is the manifested essence of God descending into conscious incarnation.

This law does not require you to feel the Flame. You can hold it as doctrine, contemplate it as a hypothesis or let it rest. Your experience rules over the map.

What descends does not come from somewhere else. It comes from a depth that your form discovers as its own.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Ask yourself calmly: can a contemplative truth survive a financial decision? Can love hold a no?

Can clarity recognize that it does not know? Can devotion live alongside tiredness, the body and the calendar?

You do not need to be perfect. Only notice where the distance between what you know and what you live is growing shorter.
