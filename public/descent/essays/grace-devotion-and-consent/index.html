Grace, devotion and consent

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch VII · Grace and the living body

The Descent · the essaysVII · Grace and body27 / 42
“Devotion opens the hands. It does not decide what falls into them, or when.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro de la Morada

Think of someone in a kitchen, in a dark room or in the seat of a car. Suddenly they are living a peace, a clarity or a presence they did not make. They do not yet know what to call it, and still, they have already been touched. Before recognizing the Monad, before speaking of the Flame or understanding the Descent, something happened.

That is how the first book began, and that is where I want to speak to you from now. We can study the quality with which the Light presents itself, prepare the house, learn to recognize a function. But before any effort, any doctrine and any human response, there is something that did not begin because we knew how to produce it.

Grace touches first.

What cannot be bought

In the Yoga of the Descent we call Grace that free self-giving of the Divine which cannot be bought, demanded or guaranteed. It is not a reward for having meditated well. It is not a spiritual salary. It comes before merit. One person may have lived a devout life and experience nothing extraordinary, and another may be touched in the middle of a confused season, with no visible preparation. I do not know why it is so, and I am not going to invent a reason for you.

That freedom protects both of them. No one can prove they are superior because something happened to them. No one can conclude they are inferior because nothing like it has happened to them. Turning Grace into a prize creates a spiritual cruelty: whoever does not receive what they hoped for ends up blaming themselves for not having been pure, faithful or disciplined enough.

Nor is every unexpected experience Grace, and Grace is not always pleasant. Sometimes it comes as comfort; at other times as a clarity that makes you look at what you would rather not see, or as a quiet strength to do what you had been avoiding for years. But we will not call harm, abuse or illness Grace. Something hurting does not automatically make it an initiation.

And if the Presence was never absent, what does it mean to say that Grace arrives? It means we are speaking from human experience. The Presence was always here, and still a day can come when you feel it near.

The heart’s yes

When Grace touches, something in you can answer. A prayer, a tear, a silence, a gratitude with no sentence, the wish to live differently. That yes of the heart, when it is sustained, is what I call devotion.

I do not define it first as an intense emotion, nor as belonging to a tradition. Devotion is orientation: the heart turning again and again toward what it recognized as true, even when there is no phenomenon, no comfort and no certainty. There are tender days and dry days, days when prayer feels alive and days when all that is left is the humble decision to sit down. If devotion depended on feeling inspired, it would be enthusiasm.

Its faithfulness is not blind loyalty to every vessel that once held it. It is loyalty to the truth those vessels were trying to host. Sometimes keeping a form is devotion. Sometimes leaving it is the most honest way to keep saying yes.

And devotion takes nothing away from Realization. “I am That” does not cancel the love for That; it can make it more intimate. There seem to be two, the soul that loves and the Divine that is loved. In the depths, perhaps it is Unity finding itself as relationship.

Opening the hands

There is a very human trap here. If devotion opens the heart, the mind immediately asks how much devotion it takes to receive a certain answer: how many prayers, how many years, what sacrifice. And so, without noticing, it turns love into currency.

A practice can prepare the conditions: silence, attention, a less agitated breath. That is real. But preparing is not producing. Opening a window lets you receive the morning better; it does not make the sun rise. That is why you should be wary of anyone who promises you a formula: “Do this for forty days and you will receive that,” “If it didn’t happen, you lacked faith.” Sentences like these turn the absence of an experience into guilt.

The same is true of the Codex. Its syllables, its breaths and its sigils can organize attention and practice. They do not buy Grace. They do not force the Monad to manifest. And saying so does not empty the practice; it frees it. A prayer can be true even if it does not change what you asked for. A vigil can have been love even if the morning comes with no perceptible answer.

Mature devotion does not bargain. It does not say to God, “I give so that you will give to me.” It says: this is what I love, this is what I turn toward, this is how I wish to live, whether or not there is a reward. And if nothing happens, it can weep, it can ask why, it can even protest. Lament can still be relationship.

Soul and body consent at their own pace

I use the word consent because the Descent is not an invasion. The Flame is not a foreign force taking over someone else’s house. In this system, the soul and the Monad are coeternal and inseparable, and consent does not create that union. It makes it possible for that union to be lived more consciously in a concrete life.

The soul does not consent to receive a stranger. It consents to live from its own depth. And no one, no dream, guide or facilitator, has the authority to announce that your soul “already consented” to something that you, as a person, do not want. That yes can be gradual. It can be renewed. Yesterday’s yes does not bind today’s body.

Because the soul can say yes in an instant, but the body keeps its own calendar. Your understanding can be complete and your breath can still grow short in the face of conflict. Your body is not contradicting the truth; it is showing you its history. That is why no practice should be used against it. Do not force your breath to prove surrender. Do not read pain as proof that you are advancing. Let medicine take care of you when it is needed: that, too, is the body’s consent. And sometimes the body’s wisest yes is a clear limit: enough for today.

To surrender, in this Yoga, is not to submit. Surrender opens the hand. Submission hands the hand over to someone else. No facilitator, partner, community or book, not even this one, can ask you for surrender as proof of purity.

No can be sacred too

That is why this teaching, which begins with a yes, has to end with a no. Without no, consent does not exist. A yes that cannot be withdrawn is no longer a yes.

An honest no protects the possibility of a true yes.

It can be no to a practice that is not right for you today, to an interpretation that does not ring true, to a teacher who demands too much, to going on when your body asks you to stop. That no does not necessarily reject Grace. It can protect the way Grace will be received. A livable house has doors. That is why in the Codex every practice includes a way out: you can stop the tone, change the syllable, close the exercise or decide not to come back.

And if a long season comes in which nothing comforts you, what I call the winter of Grace, do not take it as secret proof that you are advancing, nor as a sign of abandonment. If you are finding it hard to function, attend to it through the proper human channels before turning it into theology. Your practice can shrink without guilt. You are not alone with this.

The house can be cared for. The dawn does not sign schedules.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Reflect on this: what touched your heart before you knew how to name it?

And what do you keep saying yes to on the dry days, when you feel nothing?

If you like, say it with one small sentence: “I am willing to see whatever is true, in the measure I can live it without violence.”
