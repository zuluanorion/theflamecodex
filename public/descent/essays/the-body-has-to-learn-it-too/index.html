The body has to learn it too

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch II · Your history and your body

The Descent · the essaysII · History and body05 / 42
“The body was never the service staff. It was listening all along.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Perhaps for years you treated your body like the service staff of your spiritual life: the one that takes you to pray, the one that kneels, the one that endures the fast, the one that stays still while the important thing happens somewhere else.

And then one day, that service staff began to cry in the middle of prayer. Or to tremble. Or to breathe on its own, deeply, as if it knew something.

If this has happened to you, you already sense what I want to tell you slowly: the body was never a mere servant. It was listening. You already know that your history can express itself in patterns of the body. But there is another side. When something new begins to enter your life, your body also takes part in receiving it, in its own way, in its own language and at its own pace. It is not only the container from which you live your inner life. It also takes part in how that life is expressed, learned and woven into your ordinary days.

What the body may do

How does it take part? There is a repertoire worth knowing, not so that you go looking for it, but so that you are not frightened if it appears. Everything I am about to tell you has a “may” in front of it, and none of these things, on its own, proves anything.

Your breathing may change without your doing anything: deepening, quieting, finding rhythms of its own. To me it seems bilingual, because it speaks the language of the soul and the language of the body, and that is why it is usually the first thing you notice. Sleep may change, deeper or lighter, with waking in the small hours or with seasons when you need far more of it. Vitality may move in waves: days of a freshness that does not match how much you slept, and days of a tiredness that does not match what you did.

Sensitivity may increase, to noise, to crowds, to certain foods, to other people's emotions, as if the skin of life had grown thinner for a while. Warmth and coolness may appear, pressure and expansion, clean tears, deep stillness, gentle trembling. And movement and stillness may reorganize themselves: bodies that had been rushing for years discover they can be still, and bodies that had been rigid for years discover they want to walk, to stretch, to be on the earth.

Now, the body has a thousand reasons for doing each of these things, and many have nothing to do with your spiritual life. Sleep changes with age, with stress and with health. Sensitivity changes with exhaustion. If a change is persistent, bothersome or worrying, you see a doctor. You do not interpret it first. Describing before understanding was never a rule for the easy days. It was meant precisely for these.

Not the thermometer

And here is what I most want you to take with you, because it can save you years of confusion and comparison:

What your body feels is its response, not the measure of what arrives.

Look at it through the image of the house. Two houses receive the same dawn. In one, the curtains are light and the light comes in without drama; the change is barely noticed. In the other, the rooms had been closed for decades, and the same light raises a swirl of lit dust, creaking wood, smells that wake up. This image does not tell us who received “more” or “less.” That lies outside what we can claim. What it does remind us is that the intensity of a bodily reaction does not tell us how much Presence there was in it.

That takes apart two mistakes that cause real suffering. The first is measuring yourself upward: “her body trembles and she cries, and I only feel a quiet peace; mine must be less real.” No. A silent response can accompany a very deep process, and a spectacular response can accompany a process that is only beginning, or may not be a spiritual process at all.

The second is chasing the sensation. If you take the trembling or the warmth as proof that “it's working,” it is only a matter of time before you start looking for them, provoking them, missing them. And then the spiritual life turns into a collection of phenomena, which is exactly the opposite of the dwelling. You do not make the light. Nor do you need to make the response.

That is why I will never give you a list of “signs that the Descent is happening.” Such a list would turn the responses of your house into certificates of the light. What matters is not how much your body feels. What matters is what begins to change in your life.

When the flesh signs first

Sometimes the body responds before the mind understands what is happening. The tears come before the reason. Stillness settles in before you have decided to be still. Something changes in your chest, and only days later do you find words for what you were feeling, as if the body were reading in a language your mind is still learning.

Why it happens this way, when it does, we do not know. What I can tell you is what to do with it: do not demand that understanding arrive first. In this work, sometimes the flesh signs before the mind reads. If your body has grown still and your head does not yet know why, it is not a mistake in the order of things.

And at other times it is exactly the reverse: the mind understands years before the body dares, because the body moves at the speed at which flesh learns. Neither order is the right one. Your experience rules over the map I offer you. Always.

Caring for it while it learns

What I offer you here are not techniques, but ways of not getting in the way.

Treat it as a partner. There is an old spiritual suspicion of the body: that it gets in the way, that it weighs you down, that it distracts. I propose the opposite suspicion: on the way to the dwelling, your body is not what holds you back. It is where the dwelling is built. No one comes to live in a house by despising its walls.

Give it the basics without bargaining: eat, sleep, move, rest. It sounds too simple, and it is exactly the other way round, because in seasons of the work the basics are what hold the structure up. You do not have to see rest as a pause from the path. Caring for the body is also part of living it.

Do not force it to feel or to stop feeling. When something appears, the warmth, the tears or the gentle trembling, the instruction is the same as always: accompany first, interpret later. And keep the door to the doctor's office open. The body deserves doctors just as the soul deserves silence. Looking after your health does not interrupt your spiritual path; it sustains it.

And there are days when caring for the house looks like no practice at all. You slept badly. There was noise. You burned the dinner. You answered with less patience than you would have liked and understood nothing special. Perhaps the only sensible thing that day was going to bed early.

Sometimes the body does not need an interpretation. It needs dinner.

Not every day has to produce an experience. Sometimes holding the basics, not making things worse and coming back tomorrow is all the work there is to do. And on that day, that is enough.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Today treat your body as a partner, not an obstacle: eat, sleep, move, rest.

If something appears, the warmth, the tears or the gentle trembling, do not provoke it or hold it back: accompany first, interpret later.

And if a change persists or worries you, keep the door to the doctor's office open.
