The four phrases

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch VI · The architecture of the Dwelling

The Descent · the essaysVI · The architecture22 / 42
“The four phrases are a grammar of the Dwelling. Not a calendar of the person.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro de la Morada

Think of the difference between reading a description of your own house and seeing it one afternoon from the road, after many years away. The description may be exact, but recognition has another intimacy, another warmth. It does not bring you a new house. It gives you back the one that was always yours.

That is how I would like you to receive the four phrases that hold up the Yoga of the Descent: I am That. This is That. That lives as this. This consciously resonates as That.

They may look to you like a philosopher’s conclusions. They were not born that way. A conclusion is built: you gather ideas, compare them, put them in order and arrive at a formula. A recognition happens differently. It is something that was always present and that, suddenly, stops going unnoticed.

Keys for reading

That is why I do not offer you these phrases to convince you of anything. You can repeat “I am That” many times, and it may help you concentrate or express your faith, but it will not manufacture the recognition the phrase speaks of. The phrase does not produce what it names. It recognizes it once it has been seen, even if only for an instant.

As soon as a tool appears, so does the temptation to believe it caused everything. If a phrase touched you deeply, you want to repeat it until the same thing happens again. But these four phrases are not buttons. They are keys for reading. A key like that does not make a door exist. It helps you recognize which door is in front of you.

The only thing I ask of you is to be honest with yourself about where you are. To be able to say: this I recognize, this I understand, this I believe, this interests me, this I do not know yet. Those sentences are more spiritual than they seem. They keep your hand open. And if you do not recognize this teaching as yours, you are not left out because of it.

Before any explanation, stay for a moment with the question no one can answer for you: who says I?

I am That, This is That

We say I to speak of the body, of our history, our work and our memories. None of that is wrong: without a person able to say I, you could not care for the one you love or remember a promise. But look at yourself. Your body changed. Childhood disappeared from the photograph and yet something in you recognizes that that boy, that girl, was you. In the middle of all those changes there is a continuity that none of them manufactured: the simple fact of being, of being conscious.

I call that recognition of your deepest identity Realization. It is the moment, or the process with no single moment, in which what knows recognizes itself as prior to everything it knows. And notice this carefully: I am That does not mean your biography is the Absolute. The ego can appropriate even the phrase that was meant to put it in its place. It changes clothes, but stays seated on the throne. The phrase does not crown the character. It makes it transparent to a deeper identity.

Even so, a division could remain standing: inside, I am That; outside, the body and the world are something else. The second phrase is there so that recognition does not build you a new exile. In it, the word that decides everything is this: your body sitting as you read, the cup beside you, your tiredness, the person you love and the person you do not know what to do with. No form exists outside the ground of Being.

Nonduality erases the final separation. It does not erase difference.

And this is not a detail for philosophers. It is an ethical protection. If you and I were the same person, your consent might seem unnecessary. The unity I teach does not cancel relationship; it makes it possible. When someone tells you “if everything is One, you shouldn’t be angry,” that is not nonduality. It is using a metaphysics to abandon a concrete person.

That lives as this

Here the movement proper to this Yoga begins. The difference between is and lives looks small. It holds the whole book.

A truth can be true in being itself and not yet be embodied in the way you breathe, decide, love or respond under pressure. To live as this means to become effective in the form.

In the Yoga of the Descent we call the Divine Monad the transcendent aspect of the divine intelligence, essence and purpose of your eternal individuality. And we call the Flame the manifested essence of that Monad, the subtlest of what we are, capable of becoming consciously present in the form. That is why what descends is not a foreign energy added to the person. It is what already founds your existence, becoming consciously lived in it. Light can fill a house without every room being open.

That lives as this when Love stops being only an oceanic experience and learns to say its limit without hatred. When peace stops depending on retreat and shows up in your kitchen. Sensations may accompany this movement, but they are not gauges. What matters is not how much fire you feel. It is what begins to live in you differently. And like every work that passes through the body, it has its calendar. Realization may need no time. The flesh does.

This consciously resonates as That

When That begins to live as this, what is embodied looks for language: sound, gesture, symbol, action. There the Codex begins, and I ask you to keep this sentence in front of every practice:

The Flame brings the essence. The Codex gives it language.

This is your voice, your breath, the hand that traces, the attention that gathers and the action that follows. A syllable is not a magic password. Pronouncing it does not compel reality. It lets you organize a participation. That is why the Codex is not completed in the mouth. If you pronounce a syllable of clarity for twenty minutes and then use confusion to avoid a necessary conversation, the practice was left suspended before it reached your life.

Praying and asking keep all their dignity. But when you recognize the Flame as the manifested essence of your own Monad, your practice can also say: “I allow the quality I recognize as divine to find a more coherent form in me.” From there come the three verbs of the Codex: I resonate, I direct, I create. None of them means omnipotence. The Codex does not make you the owner of reality. It makes you responsible for the form you offer to what you say you resonate with.

A grammar, not a calendar

The four phrases gather into four verbs: recognize, include, embody and express. And they protect one another. Recognizing without including can produce a spirituality of withdrawal. Including without embodying can stay as philosophy. Expressing without recognizing can turn the Codex into technique.

Look at an ordinary scene. Someone speaks to you in a way that wakes an old defense in you. Recognizing can be remembering, even for a second: “I am not only this reaction that has just risen up.” Including, to stop treating your reaction, your body and the other person as things outside the spiritual. Embodying, to feel your feet, let the breath happen and let the truth of the moment reach your voice without turning into an attack. And expressing can be saying: “I can’t go on with this conversation this way; we can pick it up again when there is more room.” There was no vision, no ecstasy, no ceremony. And yet the four phrases found life.

They have a logical order, but life is under no obligation to read the index before it begins. Grace can touch you before any recognition. That is why it is no use saying “I am at the third phrase and you are at the first,” or forcing the next phrase. You cannot manufacture the next verb. The map becomes more useful when you stop asking “which one am I at?” and start asking “what function is asking for attention here?”

And none of these phrases authorizes anyone to declare themselves infallible, to invalidate another person’s no, to take a sensation as a spiritual certificate, or to leave medical or psychological care. If a practice becomes compulsive, increases fear or disorders your sleep, stop it and talk with someone sensible; if it persists or worries you, see a professional. The depth of an experience does not reduce responsibility. It increases it.

A metaphysics becomes habitable when it can keep you company on any ordinary Tuesday. That is why the practice of the four phrases ends with something very small and verifiable: sending the message you need to send, drinking some water, asking for help, resting. Action does not prove the metaphysics. It offers it a house.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Sit comfortably and, on each natural exhalation, say one phrase inwardly: I am That. This is That. That lives as this. This consciously resonates as That.

Do not try to feel anything. Only notice whether each phrase is, for you, recognition, belief, question, resistance or simple sound.

Stay a few moments without repeating anything, and end with one small, verifiable action: drink some water, send the pending message, rest.
