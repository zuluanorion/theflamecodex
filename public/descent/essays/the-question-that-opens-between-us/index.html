The question that opens between us

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch V · Toward others

The Descent · the essaysV · Toward others21 / 42
“What changes is not the height: it is the scale.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Think of the smallest thing we have learned to name.

A person lives with a little less fear. Not much. Just enough not to need to be right in an argument in the kitchen. Just enough to listen without preparing the answer. Just enough to say “I don’t know” without feeling they are losing something.

Sometimes, around that person, a conversation changes texture. Not miraculously: someone feels less obliged to defend themselves and says something truer than they had planned. That is all. It is little, and it does not always happen.

The Coda that closes the first volume of the Yoga of the Descent is made of scenes as simple as this one. And notice that the book does not close with one more Parte, but with a Coda.

A wider question

The Coda is not Parte XXI. It is not one more station, nor the next rung once the earlier ones are done. It is a change in the size of the question. What changes is not the height: it is the scale. No one is asking you to climb one more floor. You are invited to look wider, from your own life toward what happens between us.

For twenty Partes the underlying question was always much the same, even when it changed its clothes: what is happening to me? How do I hold this without inflating it or dismissing it? How do I live it without harming myself and without harming anyone?

It is a question that fits within one life, within yours. You can look at it, doubt it, test it, let it rest, take it to someone you trust, or wait with it at the water’s edge. The whole book is made to that measure.

But at the end of Parte XX another one appeared, and that one no longer fits within a single life:

What could happen between us?

It is not a higher question. It is a wider one. That is why the Coda carries no Parte number. If it did, it would look like the next step, as if after mutuality something greater had to come. Nothing has to come. A question simply opens.

One life, one table, many tables

Go back now to that person who lives with a little less fear, and to that conversation that changed texture.

Imagine, only imagine, because that is all I allow myself here, that it happens not at one table but at many. Not on one afternoon but over years. That it happens in families, at work, in classrooms, in waiting rooms, in neighborhoods, in those small conversations no one ever writes down anywhere.

What kind of world would begin to become possible?

I do not know. And I prefer to tell you in exactly those words: I do not know.

Horizon without prophecy

Here I want to be clear with you, because a great many things get announced in this territory.

I do not claim that humanity is entering a new stage. Nor do I claim that there is a proven collective change, or that the passing of time guarantees any transformation, or that what I have described happens on the scale of the species, the planet or biology. There is no announcement here, no date, no chosen group, no proof that anyone is ahead. Whoever is looking for that will not find it here, and I believe it is better that way.

And the reason is not only prudence. It is doctrine.

Announcing the future is the last form of ownership still standing. The whole last stretch of the book was learning not to take ownership: of the meaning an image brings, of the person you love, of the truth you think you see, of the answer the other is going to give, of what happens when two people meet. Only one renunciation was missing, and it is this one: not to take ownership of the future.

From there comes the law that guides the Coda and closes the volume:

Horizon without prophecy.

A horizon is not a promise. It is the line as far as the eye can reach from where you are standing. It moves when you walk. It is not fulfilled: it is walked toward. And to walk toward it you do not need anyone to tell you when you will arrive.

The question, in your kitchen

With that protection in place, the question can now be held without turning into something else. It is this: what could change if more and more relationships were organized from less fear, less domination, less defense and greater presence?

Do not answer it quickly. There is no need. For now, it is enough to be able to hold it.

And if someone asks what I want to propose to you, in the end, the answer fits in a paragraph. I do not claim that humanity is entering a new stage. I want to leave open a humbler question: what kind of world begins to become possible when the quality of our encounters changes.

That is all I dare to say. And it is quite a lot, because it is a question you can begin to answer today, in your kitchen, with a single conversation, without waiting for any era, without belonging to any group, without anyone having to be right.

The door that stays open

This volume was called Libro Puerta, the Door Book, for a simple reason: it never meant to be the house.

What it did was name things. It put human words where many people had only wonder, fear or silence. It told you “you are not alone with this.” It told you that a visit is not yet a dwelling, that the body has to learn it too, that a threshold does not mean something is broken, that the mind remains a tool without remaining a throne, that you can look after the house but you cannot manufacture the dawn. And in its last stretch it learned, again and again, not to take ownership. To the Coda there remained a single renunciation: not to take ownership of the future.

This book does not close with a conclusion, because it has none. It closes where it began: at a table, with someone.

If anything I have told you is of use to you, perhaps you will see it appear in small and not very spiritual things. In how you listen. In how much you need to win. In what you do when someone brings you something you cannot solve. In how you look after your limits. In how much room you leave for the other to go on being other.

They are not proofs of anything. They are simply some of the places where a life can begin to show what it is learning.

The door stays open. Not upward: toward the other side of the table.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Hold this question without answering it quickly: what could change if more and more relationships were organized from less fear, less domination, less defense and greater presence?

Begin where it can be begun today: in your kitchen, with a single conversation.

And notice how you listen, how much you need to win, and how much room you leave for the other to go on being other.
