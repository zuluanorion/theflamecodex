The regions of experience

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch III · The human map

The Descent · the essaysIII · The map08 / 42
“A region says where something becomes visible. It does not say what it is, or why, or what it means.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Listen to how you speak. How we all speak. A lump in the throat. Butterflies in the stomach. A weight on the chest. Losing your footing.

For centuries our language has linked many experiences to the body. Sometimes what we live is felt mostly in one zone, other times it is felt in a diffuse way, and other times it has no clear place in the body at all. I did not invent that link. What I offer you is not a new map. It is putting order into one you already use every day without noticing.

A map you already speak

That map only needs a little order, a few simple names and some care in the face of the two usual exaggerations: the one that dismisses it, “they're just figures of speech,” and the one that inflates it, “every emotion has its exact box.” Neither of them serves you.

Those broad zones where experience tends to make itself felt I call regions. And before we walk through them, the law that governs them: a region says where something becomes visible. It does not say what it is, or why, or what it means.

The five neighborhoods

We are going to use five broad regions. Not because the body is naturally divided into five, nor because they are the only ones possible, but because they give us a map simple enough to orient ourselves without turning the body into a game board. I walk through them from the bottom up only because one has to start somewhere, not because below is worth less or above is worth more.

The base. The pelvis, the legs, the feet, contact with the ground. Language knows it well: having your feet on the ground, losing your footing, your legs shaking. Many people notice most strongly here whatever touches on support and safety.

The belly. Butterflies, a hollow in the pit of the stomach, a knot in the gut. Many people feel fright here, the excitement of what is about to come, restlessness, and also that digestion of what has been lived.

The chest. The shrinking heart, the chest that widens, my chest was so full. Many people feel tenderness, grief, warmth or peace mostly here. And here comes the most urgent care, which I tell you very clearly: pain, pressure or tightness in the chest, above all if it is new, intense or persistent, deserves medical attention and should not be interpreted first as part of a spiritual process. The doctor's office first. The meaning, if there is one, knows how to wait.

The throat. The lump, the voice that breaks, swallowing hard, being left without words. It can draw your attention especially when your voice changes, when speaking is hard, when tears come or there is emotional tension.

The head and forehead. Clearing your head, the heavy head, the furrowed brow, seeing it clearly. Here many people notice clarity above all, that understanding that comes as if someone had cleaned a pane of glass, and also its absence, the overload of those days when everything is too much.

And there is a sixth possibility, which is not a region: that you cannot locate it. An experience can be felt in the whole body, move between several zones or have no recognizable address. You do not need to force it into the map.

None of this is an assignment. That many people notice tenderness in the chest does not oblige yours to live there. There are those who cry their grief in the belly and those who feel clarity in the whole body. The map gathers patterns that people have described, not laws. Your experience rules over the map I offer you. Always.

Three questions in one sentence

Here the pieces come together into a single tool. You now have three different questions, and none steps on the others. Which way does it seem to be heading? That is the movement. Through which function does it become visible? That is the way of response. In which zone is it most noticeable? That is the region.

With the three you can do something that at first seemed impossible: describe your moment in one sentence, without jargon, without diagnosis and without drama. Listen to how it sounds:

I'm in a season of the base: lots of ground, lots of cooking, lots of body, and my voice very quiet.

Notice what a sentence like that achieves. It says a great deal, claims nothing it should not, and anyone who loves you can understand it. It does not say “I am receiving a descent through the heart channel.” It says where, how and which way, and leaves the why in peace. That is how vocabulary works when it works well: rich description, interpretation at rest.

And notice, too, what does not get mixed up: the way is not the region. Breathing can be noticed in the chest, in the belly or in the whole body. The voice is usually noticed in the throat, but the silence of the voice can be felt in the chest. Three axes, three questions, and that is why this vocabulary describes finely without boxing anything in.

What the map does not do

Regions bring their own traps, and they are the oldest ones of this kind of map.

They are not a building with floors. There is no higher region and no lower one, no path of perfection climbing from the base to the head. Regions are not a ladder; they are neighborhoods of one city. That an experience shows in you mostly in the belly does not put you behind someone who notices it in the forehead. A whole life can unfold with great depth with its experience gathered in the base.

Nor are they a diagnostic board. The map does not give you permission for sentences like “my throat is closed” or “I still need to work on my chest.” A zone where you notice little is not a defective zone. It is a zone where, for now, you notice little. A zone is not opened or unblocked. It is inhabited.

And they verify nothing. Feeling warmth in the chest does not certify a process; not feeling it does not deny one. A region locates; it does not explain.

Some traditions developed maps of bodily or subtle centers, and certain traditions of yoga described the chakras. Those systems belong to their own contexts and are not the source of these regions. The resemblance can be studied; the equivalence is not presumed. The technical study of those correspondences belongs to the Libro de la Morada.

So the next time something moves in you, do not rush to ask what it means. Say where, how and which way. And leave the why in peace.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

If today you had to say your own sentence, in which zone would you most notice what you are living?

Through which function does it become visible to you? And where do you feel it is taking you?

Do not look for the right answer, but for the true one.
