The thresholds: the place you always come back to

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch III · The human map

The Descent · the essaysIII · The map09 / 42
“Not every “this far” is a threshold to cross. Some are limits to honor.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

There is an experience many people know and find hard to name without condemning themselves. In the middle of a life that is moving forward, even an inner life that is flowering, there is a point you reach again and again, and where, again and again, something in you says this far.

Perhaps it is the conversation with your mother that always stops at the same sentence. The closeness you can give but not receive. The decision you have known for years and still do not make. The forgiveness you understand with your head and that never comes down from there. The emotion, anger perhaps, or need, that you always, always step back from.

And along with the repetition, as punctual as it is, comes guilt: if I have grown so much in everything else, why not here? What is wrong with me?

I want to begin by taking that question apart. I call that place a threshold: a recurring point of difficulty, resistance or protection. And the first thing I have to tell you about it goes straight against the guilt.

A threshold does not mean something is broken.

Many people have places like this, which they return to again and again with a similar sense of limit. If it happens to you, there is no need to turn that repetition into proof that you have failed.

What a threshold is not

A threshold is not a zone of the body. It can become visible through one, like the throat that tightens every time you have to say no, or the belly that closes in the face of uncertainty. But the threshold is neither the throat nor the belly. The threshold is the concrete human difficulty: saying no to someone you love, staying present without certainties. The region is where it shows. The threshold is what is hard. That is why I will never tell you “your throat is closed.” I would say, if anything, “it seems that saying no to that person is still hard for you, and it shows in your voice.”

Nor is a threshold an enemy. Your defenses were not born as enemies; many began as solutions. Some thresholds may be related to learned forms of protection that keep switching on in similar situations, like a lock that stayed locked because once it was necessary to lock it. Seeing it this way changes the question at the root. You no longer ask what is wrong with you. You ask whether there is something that response is trying to protect, and what it would need in order to rest from its watch.

There are limits to honor

And a threshold is not automatically a task. It is a fine distinction, and one of the ones that protect you most in the whole book.

A threshold is a recurring point where moving forward becomes difficult. A limit is a boundary that can be necessary, healthy or protective. Saying no to what harms you is not a pending threshold. It is health. Needing time before you trust again is not resistance. It may be a prudence well earned. Because not every this far means not yet. Sometimes it means no, and that no can be health. There are limits that exist to take care of you.

And how do you tell the limit that protects you from a real danger from the one that guards an old fear? There is no formula. But there is a way to find out: without hurry, with honesty and, better still, with company. Here too, wait at the shore.

Crossing is not integrating

We live surrounded by a heroic language about limits: break your barriers, leave your comfort zone, face your fears, don’t stop. That language works for marathons. For the thresholds I am speaking of, it is usually poor advice, and sometimes harmful advice.

Forcing a limit does not guarantee that you integrate it. You can get through a situation once without anything having changed yet in what made it difficult. And when someone pushes themselves far beyond what they can hold, the experience can end in a greater overwhelm instead of a greater capacity. Closeness forced before it can be held, forgiveness demanded before it was born: getting through them once is not the same as being able to live in them.

Crossing is not integrating. Having done it once is not having embodied it.

That is why I invite you to reverse the gesture. A threshold is accompanied; it is not charged at. Some doors open on their own, with time. Others open when conditions change, when there is more safety, more support and less threat. Others open only with someone beside you. And none opens better for being kicked.

From here comes a sentence I want you to keep, because it gives dignity back to so many people who carry their not yet as a shame: “not yet” is also an answer of the path. Said honestly, the way someone says “I can’t do this yet, and I know it, and I don’t abandon myself for it,” it can be one of the most mature answers in this whole book.

But here, too, discernment is needed. Sometimes not yet is care. And sometimes, without our noticing, it becomes a way of never approaching something we truly want to live. The question is not whether you are brave or cowardly. The question is whether that not yet is caring for you or making your life smaller and smaller.

Three honest answers

When a limit repeats, it may be related to integration: something is asking for time and conditions, a protection may be ready to be relieved, a capacity is ripening. It may simply be life: strength that does not reach this year, a grief that still needs its time, a real circumstance that makes it sensible not to cross yet. Not everything that repeats is waiting to be transcended; sometimes it is waiting for life to change.

And it may need attention of its own. This I tell you without softening it: when a threshold surrounds experiences that overwhelm you, such as intense fear, memories that break in, panic or histories of harm, approaching it alone or by sheer willpower can do you real harm. That deserves professional support, not a private test of courage. Seeking professional help does not interrupt your spiritual path. With deep thresholds, very often it is the path.

The three can coexist, and none of them is decided by the intensity of what you feel.

How to live beside a threshold

I am not offering you techniques, but ways of being.

Name it without condemnation. “Here there is a threshold” is a complete sentence. It needs no continuation, no explanation and no verdict, and sometimes it is all a season allows. And that is enough.

Take the weight off the silence. If you have someone safe, putting into words what is happening to you can help you not to carry it alone, not to solve it, only so that it exists outside of you. What to tell, how much and to whom is still yours to decide.

Approach by the edges. Between charging at the threshold and ignoring it there is an enormous territory: coming a little closer, safely, and withdrawing when it is time. The difficult conversation, begun at its kindest edge. Closeness, allowed one degree. This does not mean exposing yourself on purpose to what overwhelms you: when there is trauma, panic or earlier harm, those edges are better walked with professional support.

Learn your measure. Withdrawing in time can be care, but always withdrawing can leave a difficulty untouched. The threshold does not ask you for heroism. It asks you for measure.

And let the work go on in the rest of the house. While one difficulty needs time, other parts of your life can keep growing, and sometimes it is precisely that work in the other rooms that one day changes the conditions of the closed room. Do not set up your spiritual life in front of the door that will not open.

Not everything that stops you has to be broken. Sometimes it needs to be understood. Sometimes it needs to be accompanied. Sometimes it needs time. And sometimes it is a limit that deserves to be respected.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

When you reach that place again, name it without condemnation: “Here there is a threshold.” It is a complete sentence.

Then ask yourself, unhurried: is this “not yet” caring for me, or is it making my life smaller and smaller?

You do not have to answer today. That question can be held without being resolved right away.
