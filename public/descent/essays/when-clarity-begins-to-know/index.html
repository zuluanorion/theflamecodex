When clarity begins to know

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch V · Toward others

The Descent · the essaysV · Toward others18 / 42
“Arriving first does not mean being right first.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Sometimes it happens like this. An answer appears already formed. An orientation presents itself before its reasons. You did not build it step by step. It was simply there, and your mind arrived afterward to look for words for it.

For a while that was a visit. It came now and then, without warning, and you received it with a whole toolbox of discernment around it. Further along the path something else can happen, and I want to tell you about it precisely because it is easily confused. It is not only that the visits become more frequent, although sometimes they do. It is that your relationship with knowing itself begins to change. The order that used to be the exception, first the orientation and then the examination, begins to be an ordinary way in which understanding reaches you. Not in everything, not always, not for everyone.

This is the territory where more people have lost their way than anywhere else, and where more harm has been done to others in the name of light. So I give you the law from the start, and it is never lifted: a different relationship with knowing is not a relationship exempt from error. Knowing without omniscience. Arriving first does not mean being right first.

What exactly changes

This station is not about knowing more things. It does not turn anyone into a privileged source of data, facts or information that would normally require proof. What changes, when it changes, is the way some understandings are ordered and reach you.

The orientation arrives and your mind takes it in without alarm. It looks for words for it, sets it beside what you already know, tests it, and carries it into life to see what happens. Receiving, translating, unfolding, comparing, placing in context, testing: that becomes its craft. Thought does not withdraw; it only changes its place in the orchestra. It stops being the only instrument, and it does not play any less for that. It plays differently.

And here is an honesty that protects your mind: there are whole territories where step-by-step reasoning still rules, and should rule. Numbers, contracts, medicines, road maps. The clarity of this station does not replace study, or consultation, or a sum done properly. Whoever uses it to skip them is not further along the path. They are less attentive.

In the spiritual vision I propose to you, this settling can be read as an increasingly familiar relationship between greater coherence, more inner space, reception and translation. I offer you that reading, but not as a required sequence or as a proven mechanism. Experience does not arrive labeled with its origin, not even when it becomes a habit.

What does not change

Here is the heart of all this, and I will not soften it.

Error does not retire. An orientation that arrives effortlessly can be mistaken with the same calm with which it arrives. Memory still colors things, desire still puts on disguises, fear still gets up early. That knowing changes its form does not mean its aim improves in everything.

Certainty is still a feeling, and the feeling of certainty verifies neither the truth nor the origin of an understanding. When this way of knowing becomes more familiar, the feeling of certainty can become familiar too.

And a steady river deceives more than a flood.

The inner life of another person still does not belong to you. Feeling that you know what is going on with someone does not mean you know it. However fine your reading of a tone or a pause, it is still a hypothesis of yours and not a fact about them. Orientation about your own words, your silence or your limit can be invaluable. But turning an impression about someone else’s inner life into a verdict is trespassing on a sovereignty that is not yours.

And “it came to me” is still not an argument. Whoever knows differently and uses it to close conversations has not gained clarity. They have lost the people they could talk to.

The “I don’t know” of clarity

There is a capacity this station needs to keep if it wants to remain livable: the capacity to say “I don’t know.”

It sounds contradictory, and it is not. A finer relationship with knowing also makes its edges more visible to you: this I see, this I suppose, this I wish, this I do not know yet. That is why, here, saying “I don’t know” can become more frequent and calmer, not less. You can hold open questions without rushing to close them, change your mind without feeling you are losing something, and recognize without drama the places clarity does not reach.

We all know its opposite, too: the oracle. The person who no longer asks because “it comes to them,” who has a reading for everything and a certainty for everyone, and to whom you cannot tell anything without getting a diagnosis back. That character is not this station. It is its caricature, and one of the loneliest there is. If someone finds it harder and harder to admit they do not know, that does not prove depth. It is a reason to return to discernment.

The traps of knowing differently

There are four, and all of them begin where discernment is considered finished.

The first is retiring the testing: believing that, because the orientation reaches you with more familiarity, examining it is no longer necessary. Discernment was never the toll beginners pay. It is the permanent companion. Just when knowing becomes easy is when testing matters most, because error travels comfortably too.

The second is becoming the owner of truth, gradually sliding from “this came to me” to “this is how it is,” and from there to correcting other people’s lives from your own clarity. Truth does not gain an owner by arriving faster. And there is a very homely clue: if every conversation of yours ends up as a reading of the other person’s life, people may well start telling you less.

The third is deciding for others: intervening, giving advice no one asked for, moving the pieces of other people’s lives because you “see” what they need. Feeling unity or clarity does not cancel the other’s consent or sovereignty. If it makes sense to share an impression, it is offered humbly, leaving the other completely free to recognize it, correct it or set it aside.

And the fourth is abandoning ordinary learning: no longer studying, asking, reading and listening to those who know more, because “it just comes to me now.” The old cook’s sense of direction exists because there were years in the kitchen. Receiving does not replace learning.

There are also areas where no inner clarity replaces ordinary verification: health, whether yours or someone else’s, money, legal decisions, anyone’s safety. There, the orientation you received can accompany you, but verification and professional competence come first. And if this knowing begins to give you ever larger certainties about a mission, about what others must do, or about reasons to isolate yourself from those who love you, you stop, you talk it over and, if it persists, you consult. Seeking professional help does not interrupt your spiritual path, not even when what shines is clarity.

The image taught you not to take ownership of meaning. The heart taught you not to take ownership of the other. To clarity falls the hardest lesson of all: not to take ownership of truth.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

When something reaches you with clarity, do not retire it from examination: let it pass through the four usual questions, the one of time, the one of life, the one of company and the one of the open hand.

And ask yourself the question that belongs to this station: are there still places where I say “I don’t know”?
