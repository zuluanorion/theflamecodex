When the depths speak in images

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch V · Toward others

The Descent · the essaysV · Toward others16 / 42
“The image came to your house. Your door is no one else's door.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

There are dreams you forget the moment you open your eyes. And there are dreams that have breakfast with you.

They stay with you through the day like a smooth stone in your pocket, one that does not bother you but has weight, and your hand goes back to it without asking permission. Someone tells it like this: “I dreamed of a house I didn't know, and when I woke up I still felt it was mine.” They do not always have an extraordinary plot. Sometimes it is just a door you did not open, water rising or falling, someone who is gone and was there, a fire that did not burn. And it does not only happen in sleep. In stillness too, awake, images sometimes appear that no one called: a figure, a landscape, a two-second scene, with that same weight. Some people tell of an image that begins to return, in different dreams and different moments, insistent and patient, like someone quietly waiting their turn.

That an image weighs on you does not certify its origin, its truth, its meaning or its spiritual importance. It only says that it keeps calling your attention. If you wish, you can host it.

Reflect on this: is there an image that has stayed with you like that? How long has it been with you? And did you ever let someone else decide for you what it meant? You do not need to answer now.

A very old language

Why are there moments when your inner life seems to know how to speak to you better with an image than with an explanation? The honest thing first: we do not know. But something can be observed. Images belong to a very old layer of the way we give shape to what we live. We dream, remember, imagine and tell much of what is deep through scenes, sometimes before we can explain it with concepts. And when human beings wanted to say the greatest thing they knew, they almost never said it with definitions. They said it with gardens and deserts, with floods and burning bushes, with doors, temples and roads. Not because they lacked vocabulary. Because there are experiences that let themselves be held better in an image than in a definition.

A sentence usually tries to be precise; an image can hold several associations at once, without needing to resolve them. A house can remind you of a particular house in your life and, at the same time, hold something wider about shelter, belonging or return. A door can simply be a door and, for you, also gather up a transition. None of this establishes a universal meaning. When I need to name those images that feel as if they had more than one layer, I call them deep figures, and I put no more theory on top of them than that.

That is why this language appears in the deep stages of the path. When the process touches what you learned before you had words, the oldest rooms of the house, it should not surprise us that it answers in the language spoken in those rooms.

And the honesty, complete: not every inner image belongs to a spiritual process. Images also come from memory, imagination, fear, desire, your personal history and the associations of the day. Within the vision I propose to you, another possibility remains open: that some images are one of the ways a deep reorganization becomes perceptible to us. I want to leave it open; I cannot prove it to you. A powerful image does not reveal its origin by being powerful. And if you dreamed deeply long before any dwelling, or you walk the path almost without images, nothing is wrong: your experience rules over the map I offer you, in its order too.

What an image is not

This territory drags centuries of bad habits behind it, so I will say it plainly: meaning does not come printed on the symbol. An image can hold a great deal of meaning without bringing a single translation. And there are four things an image is not.

It is not a prophecy. Dreaming something is not seeing the future, and reading images as a calendar of what is coming has produced centuries of needless fear. It is not a fact. Dreaming of someone confirms nothing about that person; it may say a lot about how you live that relationship, but it does not give you guaranteed access to the other's inner life. It is not a diagnosis. No image tells you what you have, what you lack, what block you carry or what stage you are at. And it is not an order. Of the four, this is the one that protects you most. An image can move you, insist, stay with you for years, and still it does not command. If it seems to demand that you leave someone, go somewhere, do something drastic, the urgency itself is already a reason to stop, examine and not act in a rush.

If someone offers you the meaning of your images with an owner's certainty, whether an interpreter, a community, a method or a dictionary, remember who is in charge. Not even this book can do that. For me, that is the most important chapter of your sovereignty.

How an image is hosted

If it is not interpreted as a message or obeyed as an order, what do you do with an image that weighs on you? I propose one word: you host it.

Write it down, if that helps you, or draw it, even if you draw badly. Putting it outside yourself can help you return to it without depending only on memory. Go back and look at it without demanding anything of it. Do not ask it what it means; ask it, perhaps, what it touches. What moves in you when you look at it? What does it resemble in your waking life? What do you feel in your body when it comes back? Those questions do not decipher. They accompany. And often that is enough.

Give it the time of a seed. Its meaning, if it has one and if it comes to be clear, sometimes appears in parts, when it meets things you live later. You do not pull on the stem. Nor on the dream.

Share it, if you want, with someone who knows how to accompany without taking over: a person you trust, or a therapist who knows this language. You already know the difference: “the one who accompanies asks; the one who takes over pronounces.”

And do not demand depth from everything.

Not every dream is a temple; sometimes it is dinner, the noise of the day, last night's film.

Inner life also has traffic. Some images will mean a great deal to you and others will leave without leaving much behind, and you do not have to decide that when you wake up. An image can be important without needing to be explained.

When images frighten you

We need to talk about dark images, because they come, and silence about them leaves people alone at the worst moment. Death, collapse, losses, darkness, farewells. A dark image does not have to be read literally. It may have to do with memory, fear, stress, grief, a change, or with something whose meaning we simply do not know. You do not need to resolve it in order to stop treating it as a prophecy. Nor as a fact, nor as a diagnosis, nor as an order.

And if the images will not let themselves be hosted, if they break into your day without asking permission, if they replay again and again scenes of harm you lived through, if nightmares overwhelm your sleep and your mood, if it becomes hard to tell them apart from shared reality, or if insistent images appear of harming yourself or harming someone, that is not material to interpret on your own. It deserves professional accompaniment, and soon. When an experience like this overwhelms you, the first thing is no longer to find its meaning but to receive support. Seeking professional help does not interrupt your spiritual path.

You do not have to decipher what came to your house. An image is not deciphered. It is hosted.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

If an image has stayed with you like a stone in your pocket, write it down or draw it, even if you draw badly.

Go back and look at it without demanding anything of it. Do not ask it what it means; ask it, perhaps, what it touches.

And give it the time of a seed. You do not pull on the stem. Nor on the dream.
