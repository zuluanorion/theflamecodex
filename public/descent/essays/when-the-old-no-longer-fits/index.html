When the old no longer fits

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch IV · The dwelling

The Descent · the essaysIV · The dwelling13 / 42
“Is this still necessary?”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

There are things that for years did not look like problems. They simply looked like you.

Being the strong one in the family. Saying yes before knowing whether you wanted to. Always having an explanation ready. Never asking for anything. Anticipating what others need before they ask. Playing down what is yours. No one called these patterns, not even you. They were your character, that “this is just how I am.” And they worked: they held up your world, your relationships, your place among people.

Until one day, without warning and without drama, something in all of that stops fitting. You hear yourself say the same old yes and, for the first time, you notice what it costs you. You catch yourself holding up a role that no one asked you to hold anymore. It is like putting on clothes you love and noticing they are no longer your size. They are not torn, they are not ugly, but they no longer fall where they used to.

And then a question arrives that can make you dizzy: what happens when something you called “me” for years begins to show itself as a learned way of living, one you perhaps no longer need?

Why right now

The timing is unsettling: your inner life is more settled than ever, and right now what has fit for decades comes loose. I will tell you what I see: it is not that something has broken. It is that now there is something to compare it with.

As long as a way of living is the only one you have, you do not notice it, just as you do not notice your mother tongue until you learn another. But when a quieter background appears, a space around your thoughts, the old begins to stand out by contrast. It is not purification by force. It is clarity that appears as coherence grows. The clothes did not shrink: you changed size.

Now, I owe you an honesty. Something of yours no longer fitting does not prove a Descent. Ordinary life also makes things come loose: age, a loss, therapy, a child who leaves home, a job that ends. Often several things push at once. That is why I ask you not to rush to give it a name while you are still inside it. What I offer you is not a verdict about the cause. It is a way of accompanying the loosening, wherever it comes from.

What a pattern is not

What is no longer fitting, I call a pattern: a learned way of living, a response repeated so many times that it became automatic, and so automatic that it ended up being mistaken for you. It can be a role, like the strong one, the funny one, the responsible one, the one who fixes things. It can be an inner sentence: “don't be a bother,” “you can manage on your own,” “others first.” It can be a way of protecting yourself, like keeping quiet, pleasing, controlling or hardening. Or a relationship script: who you take for granted you have to be in order to be loved.

A pattern is not a defect. It is something learned. Many began as responses that, at some point, helped you protect yourself, belong or keep going. Whoever learned not to be a bother was trying to respond to something real.

Nor is a pattern rubbish to be thrown away. Many of your patterns still serve you, and some are simply virtues with a history: the responsible one holds things that deserve holding; the one who fixes things, fixes them. Seeing a pattern is not condemning it. It is only ceasing not to see it.

And a pattern is not your essence. This is in me, but it is not necessarily who I am. The simple fact that you can observe it opens a deeper question: are you only what you learned, or is there something more essential in you from which you can see it? I will not answer it for you like someone handing you a fact. You already know my wager: that simple presence that needs no explanation in order to be.

A more respectful question

When a pattern becomes visible, the culture of personal growth usually offers a single path: work on it, break it, overcome it. I suggest you begin with a more respectful and more useful question:

Is this still necessary?

Notice that it has two dignified answers, not one. Sometimes the answer is yes. The pattern still protects something that needs protecting, or it is a way of being that harms no one and that you like to inhabit. Then there is nothing to let go of. It is respected, integrated, even thanked, with one difference: now you choose it instead of only obeying it. The maturity of this stage is not measured by how much you let go. It is measured by how much you stop confusing.

And sometimes the answer is no, or no longer. The pattern belongs to a life you no longer live, protects you from a danger that has passed, or costs you more than it gives. Then the possibility opens for it to reorganize or to be released. A possibility, not an obligation. Sometimes a pattern appears right when you reach a threshold: the threshold may be saying no to your mother; the pattern, pleasing to avoid conflict. Neither one is charged at. Both are observed and accompanied. “Not yet” is also an answer on the path, and the question that serves you is still this one: what would this need in order to reorganize without violence?

There is also a third situation, which is not an answer but a waiting: not knowing. Not knowing whether the armor is still needed, whether the role still holds something up. That doubt is not a failure of the process. It can be held without being resolved right away, and it is usually answered better in company than alone.

The old is slowly vacated

I want to tell you something that is almost never said: letting go often does not happen in a single act. It is more like falling out of use. There is no ceremony and no appointed day. The inner sentence sounds, and you no longer quite obey it. The role is offered to you, and sometimes you say no. The old reflex still jumps, and each time it finds less of the house willing to follow it.

Often the old is not torn out: it is slowly vacated.

Like a room no one uses anymore: nobody tears it down, and one day you notice you have not gone in there for months. Other times disuse alone is not enough, and you need to learn new ways of responding, set a limit, repair a relationship, or work on it with someone who knows how to accompany. That is not a failed disuse. It is disuse with help.

The old comes back in storms, and that is not a failure. Under stress, tiredness or fear, the body takes the road it knows. The measure is not whether it came back, but how long it takes to stop being your whole world.

And letting go can hurt like a farewell. Even if the pattern cost you dearly, it was yours and had been with you for years. Saying goodbye to the strong one, to the one who never asked, to the one who could always manage alone, can bring real grief, and even a kind of vertigo: if I am not this, who am I? That vertigo does not come from losing yourself. It comes from something you were wearing on top being withdrawn. It is the new space, still unfurnished. There is no need to fill it quickly.

A woman who had spent years saying yes before asking herself what she wanted tried out a small no. She spent three hours feeling guilty. Then she washed some dishes, answered two messages and went on with her day. The guilt did not prove that the limit was wrong, and the limit did not prove that everything was integrated. There was only a new response trying to learn to live in an old house.

No one decides for you what has to go

No book, starting with this one, no method, no community has the authority to tell you which parts of you must go. Your experience rules over the map I offer you, and over anyone's. Not even the Descent, as I understand it, brings a list of human parts that must disappear. What it does is increase the clarity with which we can tell the essential from the acquired.

And I ask you to take care with what runs deepest. There are patterns braided with harm you received, with intense fear, with stories that break in as soon as you touch them. That calls for professional accompaniment, not private courage. If the vertigo is no longer a visit but a state, if for weeks it is hard for you to eat, sleep, work or be with your loved ones, look for a health professional, and do it now. Seeking professional help does not interrupt your spiritual path. Often it is the most serious way of walking it.

Notice what remains. Every pattern that stops being mistaken for you leaves space behind: fewer automatic sentences occupying your inner voice, fewer roles to hold up at every hour. The house, room by room, becomes clearer. Not empty of you, but with fewer things you need to mistake for yourself.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

When you notice that a way of living is beginning to no longer fit, do not charge at it. Ask it, with respect: is this still necessary?

If the answer is yes, choose it instead of only obeying it. If it is no, or no longer, ask what it would need in order to reorganize without violence.

And if you do not know yet, hold the question without resolving it, better in company than alone.
