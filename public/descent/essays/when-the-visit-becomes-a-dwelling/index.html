When the visit begins to become a dwelling

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch IV · The dwelling

The Descent · the essaysIV · The dwelling11 / 42
“You can care for the house. You can open the windows. You can learn not to get in the way. But you cannot manufacture the dawn.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

This stage has a strange way of arriving: it can begin so quietly that very often you only recognize it when you look back.

On an ordinary day, while you wash the dishes, answer messages or are simply being you, a small thought surprises you: “it has been days without it going away completely.” The peace you used to have to wait for shows up without being invited. The inner silence that used to cost you so much is there when you wake, quietly, as if it had arrived before you. Nothing spectacular happened. Simply, at some moment you cannot point to, absence stopped being the normal thing.

And with the discovery comes, almost always, distrust: is this real, or am I telling myself a story?

Keep that question. It deserves respect, and I will give it its place at the end. First I want to name with you what may be happening.

A season, not a door

It may be that the visit is beginning to become a dwelling. The visit is an encounter; the dwelling is a way of life. And between the two there is no door you cross on a dated day. There is a season in which the experience of absence becomes less frequent, its return surprises you less, and what you received begins to take root in your concrete life.

One clarification about words, which I will make only once. I will say “it left,” “it came back,” “it withdrew,” because that is how we talk and how it feels. But what we know is not that the Presence went away: it is that it stopped being perceived. What changes, as far as I can affirm, is on the side of perception.

This stage is the silent heart of the book. Everything before it was preparing for it.

What changes and what does not

None of what follows is a requirement or a sign you have to verify. These are descriptions of what others have lived, with every “may” in the world.

Continuity changes, not intensity. This stage rarely arrives with more fireworks; it usually arrives with fewer. What grows is not the size of the moments but their seam: the spaces between one and the next grow shorter, and what was an archipelago begins to look like a coastline. If you were expecting the dwelling to announce itself with the biggest experience of your life, let me correct you with affection: intensity measures nothing.

The background changes, not always the foreground. Problems do not dissolve, your character still reacts, the thresholds are still where they were. But underneath all of that there is now a background that was not there before, or that did not stay. There can still be arguments, fear or reaction, but something in you begins to return to center sooner. And a pull toward the simple often appears: more ground, more kitchen, more body, less need to talk about all this. As if what was received, in order to stay, chose the ordinary.

And what does not change. This is not perfection: it does not make you incapable of hurting, of making mistakes or of closing rooms. And it is not a guaranteed state: there can still be days when what was staying seems absent, because the waves do not retire. That does not disprove what began.

Losing days is not losing the process.

It helps to tell apart three words that resemble each other. Rooting: what was received enters your concrete life, your gestures, your decisions, the way you treat others. Dwelling: the Presence begins to function as a background, and sometimes as a center. New normality: that new way of organizing life stops feeling extraordinary. They are not rigid steps, and they do not arrive in a guaranteed order. But neither are they the same thing.

When the center changes

There is a deeper change than the continuity of peace, and it almost never announces itself: sometimes the inner place from which you seem to live begins to change.

The mind keeps thinking. Your personality is still yours. You keep driving, working, picking up your child, stopping at the traffic light. But something prior to thought may begin to be there: a simple presence that does not need to explain itself in order to be. You can think without feeling that each thought defines who you are. You can use your mind without living locked inside it.

I am not asking you to abandon the mind. I am proposing that it go on being a tool without going on occupying the throne. It is brought down from the throne without punishment, because the throne was never its own.

And here I want to take care of you with a clarification I cannot leave unsaid. When the center changes in this way, life grows larger: you are more here, not less, more present at the table, more available to whoever is speaking to you, and very often others notice it before you do. If instead what appears is a sense of unreality, of watching yourself from outside, of not recognizing yourself or of not inhabiting your own body, that is not what I am describing, and it is not something to interpret alone. That calls for a consultation.

Without grasping it

Precisely because it is silent, this stage has its traps, and all four have something in common: they consist of grasping what only knows how to stay when it is left loose.

Watching over it is the first: measuring it every morning, asking it whether it is still there. Like sleep, stillness takes fright when you stare at it.

The dwelling is not watched over; it is lived in.

Announcing it is the second: telling it too soon, turning it into news, into identity, into a post. What is settling is grateful for a quiet table after the meal, not a microphone. Claiming it as yours is the third: believing you found the formula, that it was the word, the practice or the method, and beginning to prescribe it. You did your part, and your part was real and it is yours. But your part does not explain everything that arrived. And armoring it is the fourth: the fear of losing it turned into rigidity, changing nothing, not traveling, not exposing yourself, so that “it won’t leave.” A dwelling is not a display case.

It is accompanied with little, and on purpose, because this stage asks you to do less than any other. Keep living normally: if this is going to become a dwelling, it will have to learn to live alongside work, family, dishes, noise and the same old Tuesdays. Protect the basics: eating, sleeping, moving, resting. Leave big decisions for solid ground. And let go of comparing calendars. We do not know why this stage appears earlier in some lives than in others, and I tell you just as it is: we do not know. What we can say is what it is not: it is not a prize for the best nor a delay for the worst. Your experience rules over the map I offer you, in its timing too.

And I owe you the honesty that holds up this whole season. Nothing you did guarantees that this will happen. You can prepare the house, but you cannot turn that preparation into a formula that forces the dawn. In the spiritual vision I propose to you, that difference has a very old name: grace. “It is what comes as a gift of God’s love, and not as a payment.” That is why gratitude suits this stage better than merit.

Is this real, or am I telling myself a story?

I return to the question I asked you to keep. It is a healthy question, and it is even a good sign that you ask it. But it is not answered by looking at the sensation. No sensation certifies anything, whether it is there or missing, and interrogating the stillness, asking it “are you real?”, only produces more vigilance.

It is answered slowly, without urgency, by looking at your life. What matters is not how much the body feels; what matters is what begins to change in your life. The dwelling, if that is truly what it is, shows itself less in what you feel when you wake and more in how you are treating people. In what you no longer say in an argument. In how you rest. In the patience that surprises you first.

And look, too, at your relationship with thought. Perhaps thoughts keep arriving, but not all of them manage to take the throne anymore. It is not that you think less: it is that not everything passing through your mind has to be you.

And if, with time, life shows nothing, if there is only a pleasant sensation that touches nothing else, that too is information, and it is not a sentence. It is an invitation to go on with the ordinary process, without drama and without a verdict.

Care for the house all the same. No one waters a seed to force the spring; you water it because it is what is yours to do.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Do not take the stillness’s temperature every morning: the dwelling is not watched over; it is lived in.

Keep living normally and protect the basics: eating, sleeping, moving, resting.

And if you wonder whether it is real, look slowly at your life: how you are treating people, the patience that surprises you first.
