When understanding arrives whole

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch IV · The dwelling

The Descent · the essaysIV · The dwelling14 / 42
“That something arrives before reasoning does not mean it comes from an infallible source.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Perhaps it has already happened to you, and you did not know where to put it.

Someone asks you something difficult and, before they finish the sentence, you already know the answer. Not the wording: the answer. A problem you had been chewing on for weeks resolves itself one morning, whole, while you are doing something else, and then you spend an hour looking for the words to explain to yourself what you had already understood. Or you find yourself telling someone exactly what they needed to hear, and only as you hear yourself do you understand why it was that.

In all these cases the usual order turns around. It was not thinking and then concluding. It was understanding and then, perhaps, explaining. Your mind, which so often builds what it understands step by step, with its workshop noise, found itself receiving it ready-made, like someone who opens the door and finds the bread on the table without having heard the baker.

It is a beautiful experience, and sometimes it feels sacred. That is why I want the first thing you hear to be not its praise but its law: that something arrives before reasoning does not mean it comes from an infallible source. Keep it. Everything that follows is made so that two things can be true at the same time: what is happening to you deserves respect, and it also deserves discernment.

Why now

Perhaps you wonder why it happens to you more now. The path explains it with no added mystery. With more coherence, there is less need to believe every thought that appears. With fewer automatic sentences occupying your inner voice, there is more space. And in a house with less noise you hear more, from outside and from within. Perhaps there were always understandings that arrived whole, and the workshop of your mind was too loud to hear them.

I also owe you the usual honesty. That this happens to you more does not prove a Descent. Similar experiences appear outside any spiritual framework: when you rest, when you stop ruminating, when you gain experience in something. Nor is it a requirement or a station you have to pass through. There are whole deep paths made of patient reasoning, and they are not lesser paths. Your experience rules over the map I offer you, here too.

Where it may come from

This is where spiritual books usually cheat, with a leap that sounds pious: “if it came to me without my thinking it, it did not come from me, and if it did not come from me, it came from above.” I am not going to make that leap. There are many known ways, human and honorable, for an understanding to arrive whole.

There is the memory that does not remember having learned. You know a great many things and do not recall the first time you knew them, and sometimes that memory hands you the result without showing you the receipt. There is the quick recognition that experience gives. Someone who has been cooking for decades knows the salt is missing before tasting, and a mother knows something is wrong just from how a “hello” sounds. The reasons exist; they simply no longer need to parade. There is imagination, which also hands you complete things, and not all of them are true. There is desire in disguise, that “understanding” which happens to authorize what you already wanted to do. And there is fear in disguise, that “certainty” which happens to confirm what you already feared.

And alongside all of that, within the spiritual vision I propose to you, there is the possibility this work has been preparing since its title: that there may also be a more direct knowing, an understanding you neither build nor remember, but receive. I propose it to you as a hypothesis. I do not hand it to you as a fact. Before a concrete understanding, which reached you on a concrete morning, we do not know, and whoever promises you they know is promising you too much.

This is the point that orders everything: experience does not arrive labeled with its origin. Disguised desire and deep knowing can feel identical when they arrive. That is why the origin is not decided on arrival. It is examined afterward, discerned over time, and sometimes it stays open.

Certainty is also a feeling

There is something uncomfortable to say: these understandings usually arrive with a seal of certainty. You do not feel them as hypotheses but as evidence, precisely because you cannot see the seams of thought in them.

Feeling something as true is not knowing that it is true.

And feeling it as divine, feeling that it comes to you from God, does not prove that its origin is divine. History is full of people who felt, with complete certainty, things that harmed them or others, and full too of true understandings that arrived shyly, with no seal at all. The intensity of an “it came to me” does not measure its truth.

From this comes a simple prudence for how you deal with others: “it came to me” is not an argument. In a conversation, in a couple, in a community, what you understood this way enters the dialogue as something of yours that you offer, not as a card that wins because it comes from above. Whoever uses what they received to close conversations is not sharing a knowing. They are armoring a position.

Reflect on this: were you ever completely sure of something that, with time, turned out not to be true? And did something ever reach you shyly, with no seal, that over the years turned out to be true? Look at it without judging yourself.

The mind changes trades

If all of this ended in a “stop thinking and trust,” I would be betraying the whole book. The mind does not lose its job. It changes trades. Before, it seemed its task was to build every understanding step by step; now it can also learn to receive and to translate. It can put words to what arrived without them, unfold it, compare it with what you already know, examine it, or discover, while translating it, that there was nothing to translate. That translation is real and noble work, a goldsmith's labor: receiving in an instant what then takes months to say.

Whatever arrives, receive it without obeying it in the heat of the moment. Write it down, give it its place and give it time, because waiting can help you tell what still makes sense from what was born of the intensity of the moment. Big decisions are grateful for firm ground, above all when something arrived with the feeling of revelation.

Discernment does not retire

Right where something sounds like revelation is where discernment works hardest. Not where it retires. I leave you four questions. None of them proves the origin; together, they help you discern.

The first is time: let a few days pass and ask yourself whether it still makes sense now that the intensity has gone down. The second is life, the great question of this book: when you bring it into your life, do honesty, responsibility, contact with reality, care for yourself and for others grow? Or does it produce isolation, grandiosity, secrecy, urgency, constant exceptions for yourself? The third is company: can you share it with someone sensible without needing them to accept it as a revelation? And the fourth is the open hand: can you let it be examined and put to the test without defending it as if it were part of who you are? If an understanding does not tolerate questions, if it demands immediate obedience or secrecy from you, that intolerance already tells you something, and it is not good.

And one prudence I do not want to be lost. If what arrives orders you to isolate yourself from those who love you, to harm yourself, to harm someone, or to hand over what protects you, that is not something to discern alone with a book. That calls for consulting someone, and now. And if what arrives is not quiet but insistent, if it frightens you, if it comes as voices or orders you cannot let rest, you deserve to be seen by a health professional, soon and without shame. Seeking professional help does not interrupt your spiritual path. Discernment includes knowing when the question is no longer a spiritual one.

Remember both things at once: what is happening to you deserves respect, and it also deserves discernment. What you receive is held better in an open hand than in a fist. As a question, not as an order.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

When something reaches you whole, receive it without obeying it in the heat of the moment: write it down, give it its place and give it time.

Then bring it through four questions: time, life, company and the open hand.

And reflect on this: were you ever completely sure of something that, with time, turned out not to be true? Look at it without judging yourself.
