When what you received keeps working

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch IV · The dwelling

The Descent · the essaysIV · The dwelling15 / 42
“A piece of data is stored; a seed unfolds.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Some people describe something that is very hard to put into words. Something reached them in an instant: an understanding, or sometimes not even that, just an especially deep silence, a moment they could not name. And it never finished arriving. Months later they find it at work in places that had nothing to do with it: a work decision that sorted itself out, a way of arguing that changed without their setting out to change it, priorities no one revised by hand that woke up in a different order. It is as if what entered in an instant kept unfolding inside, slowly, touching rooms of the house where no one had put it.

They do not describe it as an effort. They tell it with strangeness and with modesty, because said out loud it sounds odd:

It wasn't that I understood something and applied it. It was as if what I understood kept understanding me.

I want to give that experience a place where it can fit, without inflating it and without dismissing it. And because the ground is delicate, I put two laws in front. You already know the first: that something arrives before reasoning does not mean it comes from an infallible source, and that holds as much for what arrives in an instant as for what unfolds over years. The second holds up everything else: what you received does not make you special. If anything, it makes you responsible.

The ordinary explanation, first

Before the hypothesis, the honesty. There is an ordinary and beautiful explanation for almost everything I have just described: this is how any important understanding works. Whoever understood something deep about forgiveness, or about their father, or about fear, goes on discovering for years consequences they did not see on the first day. Life itself can also reorganize our priorities: grief, love, age, a loss or a new responsibility can change for years the way we live. And what we understood in one place of life goes on applying itself in others, without asking permission. We call that maturing.

None of this needs any other explanation. Experience does not arrive labeled with its origin, not even when the unfolding lasts for years. Whoever assures you that your slow reorganization proves a higher source is selling you the label that experience does not carry.

So why go on? Because in this book I hold, from its very title, a spiritual vision, and I want to tell you what it proposes here, with exactly the weight it has: as a proposal, not as a fact.

A piece of data and a seed

Let us begin with one of those distinctions you understand in the kitchen. There are things that are received and stored, like a piece of data, a date or an instruction. They take up their place and stay there, complete from the day they arrive. And there are things that are received and unfold. A seed is not the description of a tree kept in a drawer. It is something that, placed in the soil, can unfold a form we cannot yet see whole at the beginning.

The hypothesis I propose to you, and I mean the word propose, is that on the path of the Descent there may be receptions of the second kind. That some of what arrives in stillness may not be only an understanding the mind translates and files away, but something more like a seed, received in an instant and unfolded over a lifetime. Not information that informs you. Information that goes on forming you. And forming, here, does not mean programming your biology. It means that a received understanding could keep influencing the way you perceive, choose, relate and organize your life. The seed is an image, not an explanation of the mechanism.

I leave you three honesties where they cannot get lost. We do not know: no one can point to a concrete reorganization in a concrete life and certify where it came from. You do not need to believe it: everything we have walked through works just the same if this hypothesis is one too many for you, and you can leave it on the table and walk the whole path without it. And it does not describe a mechanism. I do not know how that would happen inside your body or your mind, and I am wary of anyone who claims to know.

Soil, time and watering

If the hypothesis were true, and hold it as a hypothesis as you read, how would one accompany an unfolding like this? You already know, because we have been learning it from the start under other names.

A seed does not unfold in the air. It needs soil. And the soil, here, is ordinary life, the usual one: work, people, the dishes, ordinary Tuesdays. If what you received is going to form something, it forms it there, in how you treat others, in what you decide, in what you no longer say in an argument. That is why rootedness was never a stage for beginners to be left behind. It is the ground where any depth becomes real. Without that encounter with life it is very hard to tell what is being integrated from what is imagination or desire.

And a seed is not hurried. You can tend the house; you cannot manufacture the dawn. Nor do you pull on the stem to make it grow. The unfolding, if it happens, keeps its own calendar, and forcing it can end up hindering the very integration you are seeking. What is yours to do is to water, not to force the spring, but because that is what the one who lives there does.

Your mind is not left without a trade either. Its translation becomes long-breathed: putting words, months later, to what changed without announcing itself, recognizing the new order when it appears and, this matters, telling what is truly unfolding from what you would simply like to unfold.

The traps that touch who you are

This ground has the most costly traps, because they touch your identity. There are three, and I will not soften them.

The first is believing yourself special: turning what you received into rank. “It was given to me, so I am a channel, I am chosen, I have a mission the others do not have.” That is where spiritual hierarchies come from, and leaderships that allow no questions. The vaccine is the second law, said in full. If something was given to you, it was not given to you as a medal. If something was given to you, it will show in what you give: in patience, in honesty, in care. And if a supposed reception begins to produce in you superiority, the feeling of being the exception, or contempt for others, stop and return to discernment.

The second disguises itself as humility: signing everything in ink from above, renaming as “received” every idea of your own, every preference, every decision, until “it came to me” replaces “I want” and no one can talk with you anymore. “It came to me” is not an argument, and it is not a signature either. Most of what you think is still yours, made in your usual workshop, and that does not make it any less worthy. It makes it something that can be talked about.

The third is to stop living while you wait for the next delivery. If what matters arrives on its own, why decide, study, work or make mistakes? This is how the most fruitful hypothesis becomes the most barren excuse, a life in the waiting room. But a seed without soil does not unfold, and the soil is worked with a whole life: decisions made, mistakes made, patient reasoning.

The four questions of discernment stretch here to the measure of years: time, life, company and the open hand. And a great inner reorganization, wherever it comes from, can destabilize you. If it is overwhelming you instead of ordering you, if it brings a grandiosity that frightens those who know you, drastic decisions one after another, or the growing certainty of a mission that sets you apart from everyone, seek a professional's eyes soon. Seeking professional help does not interrupt your spiritual path. At this depth, it may be what saves it.

Receiving does not replace living. In the vision I propose to you, receiving is for living.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Water the usual soil: work, people, the dishes, ordinary Tuesdays. That is where something received becomes real.

Do not pull on the stem. What unfolds, if it unfolds, keeps its own calendar.

And over the years, ask yourself whether what you call received shows in what you give: in patience, in honesty, in care.
