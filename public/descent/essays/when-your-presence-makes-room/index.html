When your presence makes room for others

The Yoga of the Descent · Branch V · Toward others

The Descent · the essaysV · Toward others20 / 42
“Presence does not create owners.”From Yoga del Descenso · Libro Puerta

Let us begin with what you may already have lived, from the side of the one who receives it.

There are people beside whom you notice that you breathe more slowly, that you lower your guard without having decided to, that you speak with more sincerity than you had planned. You feel seen without feeling invaded. The body loosens. You can stay silent without discomfort. You hear yourself better, as if your own inner voice had, right there, better acoustics. And sometimes you remember something about yourself you had forgotten.

Perhaps that person did nothing noticeable. They did not teach you, did not advise you, made no memorable gesture. And yet something in the quality of the encounter seemed to make a difference.

Reflect on this: who have you been with when something like this happened to you? What was their way of being with you? And did they ask you for anything in return?

The human comes first

Before any hypothesis, let us say what is human, because the human is already a great deal. We human beings affect one another in relationship. Sometimes feeling safe, heard or accompanied by someone can make it easier for the body to lower part of its guard. A calm and available person can help an encounter become calmer. It does not always happen, nor does it happen to everyone in the same way.

Part of this is sometimes called co-regulation, and that is ordinary human language. Those everyday words already explain a great deal about these encounters, and I do not want to skip over them. Nothing in this layer, by itself, proves anything beyond the human.

What I want to ask about is what there may be in addition. And the law comes first, because it closes a whole arc of the book: presence without ownership. The image taught you not to take ownership of meaning; the heart, not to take ownership of the other; clarity, not to take ownership of truth; expression, not to take ownership of the other’s response. To presence falls the last lesson: not to take ownership of what happens in the encounter.

The candle

On that human ground I open a possibility for you, and I open it as what it is: a hypothesis, not a fact.

Within the spiritual vision we have been exploring, the possibility remains open that a person whose life has become more coherent with the Presence might contribute to a quality of encounter in which another person more easily recognizes something of their own stillness or depth. Not as a substance someone possesses and transmits. Not as something that leaves one person and enters another. But as a quality of the encounter.

To say it, I choose a homely image: the candle. Two people walk into a room where a candle is lit. The candle does not push its light toward anyone. It does not convince, does not initiate, does not command, does not convert. It simply burns, and because it burns there is more light available in the room. What each person manages to see is still their own.

That is why there is a sentence you will never read here: “I give you the light.” The furthest this hypothesis goes is this other one:

“In this encounter there is enough light for both of us to see.”

And even that I tell you with three honesties. We cannot prove it. The experience of the encounter does not arrive labeled with its origin. And nothing I propose here describes a mechanism, whether energetic, biological or of any other kind.

A living relationship does not run one way

If light does not work as anyone’s property, the oldest division of roles in spiritual life falls apart: the one who gives and the one who receives.

The deep encounter happens between people, presence with presence, human with human. There may be a real difference between the two: one may have more years, more lived silence, more road behind them. But that difference does not make anyone the owner of the truth. Someone can have walked further without becoming the owner of the path. And the one who seems more settled can also be touched, learn, remember and change, sometimes more than the other, and will not always know it.

Mutuality does not mean that everyone plays the same role. When someone accompanies, teaches or holds a space, having more experience can bring them more ethical responsibility, not more rights over the other person’s life.

So I propose a change of place: the center is no one. Not “look at me,” but “let us look together at what becomes visible here.” Not “I know who you are,” but “perhaps my presence can help you hear yourself better.” The center of the encounter is not the personal authority of whoever seems to know more. It is the quality of the shared presence.

Presence does not create owners

The history of spirituality is full of the absence of this law, and that is why I cannot leave it out.

Every idea of a “presence that helps” can degenerate into the same thing: a cult of the person, dependence, hierarchy and obedience, and from there, with a very sad frequency, into abuse of power, of money, of the body and of trust. The sentences of that degeneration are well known, and I write them here so that you can recognize them: “I know what you need,” “if you doubt, it’s your ego,” “I transmit the light to you,” “without me you cannot get there.” When sentences like these are used to invalidate your judgment, demand your obedience, isolate you or override your limits, they are an alarm, not a sign of depth.

No experience of peace, clarity or openness in another person’s presence hands that person authority over your life. Not the most beautiful. Not the most repeated. That someone helps you hear yourself does not give them the right to speak in your name.

And the law has two faces, because you can be on either side. If you are the one receiving, be grateful for the encounter and keep your judgment, your bonds, your money and your life. If you are the one accompanying, what someone feels in your presence does not prove that you produced that state, and if people begin to attribute powers or special authority to you, that attribution does not become true by being sincere. If they try to seat you on a throne, do not sit down.

No one sees for you

What you feel beside someone does not verify that someone. There are people beside whom beautiful states are lived and who, even so, do harm. An encounter is discerned like everything else on this path: by watching over time how that bond lives alongside your life, in company and with an open hand.

You do not need to chase these experiences or turn certain people into their source. If these encounters exist, they are a gift of the path, not its goal. Going out to hunt for “high-presence” people, collecting closeness, measuring teachers: all of that is the old pursuit of states with new protagonists. The house is cared for, the dawn is not manufactured, and no one can sell you the way in.

Near a clearer life it may be easier to see clearly. That is the whole hypothesis. But the seeing is still yours, the path is still yours, and no encounter replaces your own process.

There is one more door I want to leave open, as a possibility and not as a prophecy. If something in this hypothesis describes a real possibility, perhaps teaching, tomorrow, will not consist of passing on answers but of creating encounters where truth can be recognized without an owner. We do not know whether it will be so. But I can confess my aim to you: not to be needed. Not to look for followers, but for people more able to listen to themselves; not to leave disciples, but after-dinner tables where people could speak truthfully. Sometimes you teach by what you say. And sometimes, without teaching anything, your way of being changes the room.

The candle does not need to convince the room that there is light. It only needs to be lit.

FOR YOUR PRACTICE

Reflect on this: who have you been with when something like this happened to you? What was their way of being with you? And did they ask you for anything in return?

Look for the answer within yourself, calmly.

And if there is someone near whom you live something beautiful today, watch carefully whether your world, beside that person, grows larger or keeps shrinking toward them.
